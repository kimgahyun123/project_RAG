5.7.4 Checklist: Commonwealth Complicity Type of Offence in Issue

Four elements the prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt:

1. 1. Someone committed the offence charged; and

2. 2. The accused actually assisted or encouraged the principal offender to commit the offence; and

3. 3. When the accused assisted or encouraged the principal offender to commit the offence, s/he had the necessary state of mind; and

4. 4. The accused did not effectively terminate his/her involvement before the offence was committed.

1. Offence committed

5. 1. Did someone commit the offence charged?

1. Consider – Has the prosecution proven all elements of that offence?

1. If Yes, then go to Question 2

2. If No, then the accused is not guilty of aiding or abetting the offence charged

2. Actual assistance or encouragement

6. 2.1 Did the accused do something that helped the principal offender to commit the offence?

3. If Yes, then go to Question 3.1

4. If No, then go to Question 2.2

7. 2.2. Did the accused do something that encouraged the principal offender to commit the offence?

5. If Yes, then go to Question 3.1

6. If No, then go to Question 2.3

8. 2.3. Did the accused do something that conveyed to the principal offender, by words or presence or behaviour, that s/he supported the commission of the offence?

7. If Yes, then go to Question 3.1

8. If No, then the accused is not guilty of aiding or abetting the offence charged

2. Consider – What effect did the accused’s words, presence or behaviour have on the principal offender?

3. State of mind

9. 3.1 At the time the accused assisted or encouraged the principal offender, did s/he know, or believe in, the essential circumstances that make up [intended offence]?

9. If Yes, then go to Question 3.2

10. If No, then the accused is not guilty of aiding or abetting the offence charged

10. 3.2 Did the accused intend to assist or encourage the principal offender to commit an offence of the same type as the offence that s/he has been charged with aiding or abetting?

11. If Yes, then go to Question 4

12. If No, then go to Question 3.3

11. 3.3 Was the accused aware that there was a substantial risk that the principal offender would commit the offence charged?

13. If Yes, then go to Question 3.4

14. If No, then the accused is not guilty of aiding or abetting the offence charged

12. 3.4 Was it unjustifiable for the accused to take that risk, in the circumstance known to him/her?

15. If Yes, then go to Question 4

16. If No, then the accused is not guilty of aiding or abetting the offence charged

4. Termination

13. 4.1 Did the accused terminate his or her involvement in a timely manner, before the offence was committed?

3. Consider – How long before the offence was committed did the accused withdraw his/her assistance/encouragement?

17. If Yes, then go to Question 4.2

18. If No, then the accused is guilty of aiding or abetting the offence charged (as long as you have also answered Yes to questions 1, 2, and 3)

14. 4.2 Did the accused take all reasonable steps to prevent the offence being committed?

4. Consider – What steps did the accused take? In the circumstances, were those all the steps s/he could reasonably take?

19. If Yes, then the accused is not guilty of committing the offence charged by pursuing a joint enterprise

20. If No, then the accused is guilty of aiding or abetting the offence charged (as long as you have also answered Yes to questions 1, 2, and 3)
